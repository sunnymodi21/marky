How it works

Copy. It converts. Paste.

No extra window for conversion. Marky sits in your menu bar, recognizes Markdown the instant it lands, and files every copy — text and screenshots — into searchable history.

1

Copy anything

Grab Markdown from Claude Code, Codex, another terminal agent, a README, or your notes with a normal ⌘C. Screenshots and images are saved too. Commands, URLs, and source code pass through unconverted.

2

Marky rewrites the clipboard

When the copy is Markdown, the clipboard gets rich text and styled HTML — while the plain-text side keeps your original Markdown, character for character. Nothing is ever destroyed.

3

Paste anywhere — or from history

Rich editors like Google Docs, Slack, Notion, and Mail get formatted text. Terminals still get raw Markdown. Press ⌥⌘V anytime to search earlier clips and paste one into the app you came from.

Clipboard history

Win+V, for your Mac

Every copy is saved locally — text, images, screenshots. Open a floating, keyboard-first window, arrow through clips, hit Return, and the clip pastes into the app you were using. Copied a screenshot? Extract the text with on-device OCR.

Text and screenshots

History records what you copy, not just Markdown. Images and macOS screenshots (TIFF) are stored as PNG with thumbnails. Search covers the full remembered list, not just the last few.

Keyboard-first, paste on pick

⌥⌘V opens the window. ↑/↓ to move, Return to paste, Esc to dismiss. With Accessibility granted, Marky reactivates the previous app and presses ⌘V for you — like Windows clipboard history, on a Mac.

OCR any screenshot

From history, extract text from a copied image with Apple's on-device recognition. The result lands on your clipboard as editable text. The image never leaves your Mac.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Marky a clipboard manager?

Yes. It records text and images as you copy them, persists history on your Mac, and opens a searchable floating window with ⌥⌘V. Pick a clip and it pastes into the app you came from. Markdown conversion and screenshot OCR sit on top of that same history.

Does it work with Claude Code and Codex?

Yes. Copy their Markdown output from the terminal as usual. Marky detects and formats it for rich-text apps while keeping the original Markdown available for terminals, editors, and developer tools.

Can it read text from screenshots?

Yes. Copy a screenshot or any image, open history, and run OCR on that clip. Recognition uses Apple's on-device Vision engine — nothing is uploaded. The extracted text is copied to your clipboard so you can paste or edit it.

Does my clipboard ever leave my Mac?

No. Detection, conversion, history, and OCR all run on-device. Marky makes no network requests, and your history is a local file you can delete at any time.

Will it mangle things that aren't Markdown?

Marky scores each copy before touching it. Bare URLs, shell commands, source code, and very long texts are deliberately left alone. And even when it does convert, the plain-text version of the clipboard keeps your original characters — so a "wrong" conversion never loses data.

What about passwords and sensitive copies?

Anything marked concealed or transient by password managers (the nspasteboard.org convention used by 1Password and friends) is skipped entirely — never converted, never recorded in history.

Why does Marky ask for the Accessibility permission?

Only the history window's paste-on-pick uses it, to press ⌘V into the app you came from. Decline it and everything else still works — picking a clip simply copies it for you to paste yourself.

What does it run on?

macOS 15 Sequoia or later. It's a tiny menu-bar app — no dock icon, no login nags, a few megabytes of memory.
